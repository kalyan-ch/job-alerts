Week 0 — Admin, before any studying

Open with these — practice loops

  • Microsoft — coding bar more forgiving, varies by org. Final "as appropriate" round with a senior leader.
  • Amazon — coding usually LC medium; Leadership Principles decide the loop. Good first loop if stories are ready by week 5.
  • Apple — team-run, domain depth over LC volume. Loop tracks the team's actual product.

Hold these until coding is warm (week 6+)

  • Meta — ~2 mediums per 45-min round. Speed is the filter, not difficulty.
  • Google — one problem per round with escalating follow-ups; graphs/DP more common. Packet goes to hiring committee, then team match. Apply early anyway — schedule the screen for week 6+. Team match after HC can take months and sometimes ends with no offer: keep other loops running, don't hold offers waiting on it.
  • Netflix — few senior openings, heavy culture rounds, design leans on resilience. Go when stories are polished.
From ~100K interviews: technical ability did not obviously track success — practice count did. Only ~25% of candidates perform consistently interview to interview. Treat the first wave as paid practice.

Coding — 70 hrs, 1 hr/day, weeks 1–10

Runs in parallel with every other block. Don't batch it. Problem list: NeetCode 150, in roadmap order.

Daily hour: 1–2 new problems + 1 cold redo from 2 days ago. 25-min timebox per new problem, then NeetCode's video. That's ~10 new/week, ~100 total — enough for Tiers 1–3 in full (107). Tier 4 only if ahead. A category box means the pattern clicks, not every problem done. Weeks 8–10: 2 of the 7 hours become mocks.

System design — 70 hrs, 1 hr/day, weeks 1–10

Phase 1 — primitives, weeks 1–2 (~14 hrs)

Phase 2 — prompts, weeks 3–8 (~42 hrs, 2 per week)

Each prompt = 3 days. Day 1: read a reference (Hello Interview, or Jordan Has No Life on YouTube). Day 2: design it cold, out loud, 45-min timer. Day 3: Phase 3 defense drill on your own design.

Weeks 9–10 — timed mocks (~14 hrs)

Phase 3 — defense drill (day 3 of every prompt)

Per-company flavor

  • Meta — you drive; interviewer follows. Staff signal is picking the one hard part and going deep, not covering every box. E6 loops often carry a second design round.
  • Google — numbers first, then design. Expect deep dives on storage and sharding choices. Prompts often mirror Google products.
  • Amazon — LP questions inside the design round. Talk operations: alarms, dashboards, failure blast radius, cost.
  • Netflix — availability over consistency, multi-region, graceful degradation, chaos testing. Name what fails and what the user sees.
  • Apple / Microsoft — team-dependent; often grounded in the team's real system. Ask the recruiter what the team owns.
Classic distributed systems is the round you pass or fail on at big tech. ML system design only if the role is ML-adjacent — confirm with recruiter. Skip DDIA outside ch. 5–9.

Behavioral — 12 hrs, weeks 4–8

Scored axes

  • Scope of impact — one team / multi-team / org-wide
  • Ambiguity tolerance
  • Influence without authority
  • Judgment under tradeoffs, second- and third-order thinking
  • Self-awareness about failure

Company-specific

Technical gets you hired, behavioral sets your level. An ex-Meta hiring committee chair reads behavioral feedback first on staff packets. Down-level cost ~20–30% comp. Plain STAR breaks at staff scope — stories span months and multiple workstreams.

Code quality & dry-run — 6 hrs, weeks 6–7

Grading axis: juniors flag style, seniors flag correctness risk and rank findings by impact. Clean variable names and helper functions count at senior level — working-but-messy reads as mid-level. CoderPad 2026: code review 21%, debugging 19% of assessments.
